The Domino Chain

How one animal destroys an entire coastline

STEP 1 OF 7

Nutria eat marsh grasses — roots and all

Unlike other herbivores that nibble the tops, nutria dig up and devour the entire root system. The whole plant is destroyed, not just trimmed.

💭 What happens to soil when there are no roots left?
STEP 2 OF 7

No roots = nothing holding the soil together

Roots are like tiny anchors — millions woven through the mud, gripping it in place. Pull out every anchor and the soil has nothing keeping it from washing away.

💭 What does water do to loose soil with no anchors?
STEP 3 OF 7

The soil dissolves. Marsh becomes open water.

Tides and rain wash the unprotected soil away. What was once solid ground becomes a shallow lake. Scientists call this an "eat-out."

💭 Who used to live in that grass before it disappeared?
STEP 4 OF 7

Baby fish, crabs, and shrimp lose their hiding places

Marsh grass was a nursery — young animals hid among the stems. No grass means nowhere to hide. They get eaten, or leave. Populations crash.

💭 What happens to the animals that ate those fish?
STEP 5 OF 7

Birds and predators starve

Herons, egrets, pelicans — all depended on fish and crabs. Now they have no food source. The entire food web unravels from the bottom up.

💭 The marsh did one more job. It protected something…
STEP 6 OF 7

Remove the sponge — cities flood

Marshes absorbed storm surges like a giant sponge, breaking waves before they hit cities. That buffer is gone. When hurricanes hit, nothing stops the water.

💭 All of this… from one animal?
STEP 7 OF 7

One animal. No predators. Total collapse.

Every link in this chain was connected. Pull out the first piece — the grass — and everything else falls.

⚠️ Louisiana loses a football field of wetland every 100 minutes.
