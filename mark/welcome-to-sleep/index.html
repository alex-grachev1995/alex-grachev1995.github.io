Welcome to the Sleep Lab!

When you close your eyes at night, you might think your brain just... turns off. Like a light switch.

But that's NOT what happens!

Your brain and body go on an amazing journey through 4 different stages every single night. Each stage does something different and important.

Ready to explore what's REALLY happening while you snooze?

⏱️ Lasts 1–7 minutes
Also called: N1 • NREM Stage 1 • Light Sleep

Stage 1 (N1): Falling Asleep 😴

This is the moment you drift off. Your eyes close, your breathing slows down, and your muscles start to relax.

But you're not fully asleep yet — you're in that fuzzy zone between awake and asleep. If someone said your name, you'd probably still hear them!

Your brain waves start getting slower, like a car gradually taking its foot off the gas pedal.

⏱️ Lasts 10–25 minutes
Also called: N2 • NREM Stage 2

Stage 2 (N2): True Sleep 💤

Now you're properly asleep! Your body temperature drops, your heart beats slower, and your muscles relax even more.

Your brain sends out special little bursts of energy — like a "Do Not Disturb" sign — that help block noises from waking you up.

You spend about half of your whole night's sleep in this stage! That's more than any other stage. N2 shows up in every single cycle and keeps getting longer as the night goes on — that's how it adds up to so much.

⏱️ Lasts 20–40 minutes
Also called: N3 • NREM Stage 3 • Deep Sleep • Slow-Wave Sleep • Delta Sleep

Stage 3 (N3): Deep Sleep 🛏️

THIS is the good stuff. Deep sleep is when your body does its most important repair work. Your muscles grow, your immune system gets stronger (that's what fights germs!), and your energy refills for tomorrow.

It's REALLY hard to wake someone up during deep sleep. And if you DO get woken up, you'll feel super confused and groggy — like your brain is stuck in mud.

Your brain produces very slow, large waves called delta waves — that's why this stage has so many names.

⏱️ Lasts 10–60 minutes
Also called: REM Sleep • Rapid Eye Movement Sleep • Paradoxical Sleep

Stage 4: REM — Dream Time! 🌈

REM stands for Rapid Eye Movement. During this stage, your eyes zip back and forth under your closed eyelids — super fast! Your brain becomes almost as active as when you're AWAKE. That's why some scientists call it paradoxical sleep — "paradox" means something that seems to contradict itself (asleep body, awake brain!).

This is when the most vivid, wildest dreams happen. But here's the weird part: your body becomes temporarily paralyzed. You can't move your arms or legs! Why? So you don't act out your dreams and accidentally hurt yourself.

REM sleep is also when your brain files away everything you learned during the day — like sorting papers into folders. Skip REM, and that stuff doesn't get saved!

🔄 Sleep Cycles — All Night Long!

You don't go through these 4 stages just once. Your brain repeats the whole cycle over and over — about 4 to 6 times every night!

Each cycle takes about 90 minutes (1 hour and 30 minutes).

N2 shows up in every cycle and gets longer through the night — that's why it adds up to about half of all your sleep!

That's why you usually remember dreams from right before waking up — you're in a long REM stage!

⚠️ Not All Stages Are Good for Waking Up!

Ever been woken up and felt TERRIBLE? That's because you were probably in the wrong stage.

😊
N1 & N2
Light sleep — you'll feel fine!
😵
N3
Deep sleep — you'll feel AWFUL and confused
😕
REM
Dream sleep — you might feel weird and groggy

The BEST time to wake up is between cycles — when your body naturally surfaces into light sleep. So if each cycle is 90 minutes, waking up after a complete cycle means you wake up feeling great!
