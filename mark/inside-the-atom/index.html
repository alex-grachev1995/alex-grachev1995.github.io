Inside the Atom

Everything in the universe — your body, the air, the Sun, a nuclear bomb — is made of tiny atoms.

Today we're going to look inside them and find out where nuclear energy comes from.

Part 3: The Glue — Strong Nuclear Force

+
STRONG FORCE

The strong nuclear force is the strongest force in nature — but it only works at incredibly tiny distances (the width of a proton). It's the "glue" that holds the nucleus together despite the protons pushing apart.

Part 4: Iron — The King of Stability

Binding energy per nucleon — how tightly each particle is held. Iron (Fe) sits at the top.
Iron has the most tightly-packed nucleus of all. On this graph it sits at the very top. Moving toward iron releases energy. Trying to move away from iron costs energy.

Both reactions need energy to get started — but they release much more than they take. Fission just needs one neutron. Fusion needs millions of degrees of heat. That's why we have fission power plants but not yet fusion ones.

Part 8: Nature's Own Nuclear Reactor

The Oklo uranium mine in Gabon, Africa — site of a natural fission reactor
1.7 billion years ago in Gabon, Africa, a uranium deposit became a natural fission reactor. Groundwater slowed the neutrons just enough to keep a chain reaction going — for hundreds of thousands of years.
