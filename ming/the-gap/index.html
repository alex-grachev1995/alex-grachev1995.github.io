THE GAP

The population explosion is not a line. It is the space between two lines.
Everybody draws the population explosion as one rising curve. That curve is real, and it explains nothing at all.
Population is the difference between two other numbers: how many people are born each year, and how many die. Nothing else. If those two numbers match, population is flat — no matter how enormous they both are.
So the explosion is not a curve. It is the gap between two curves, and everyone alive today is standing in it.
Four acts. In the first you watch a world with no medicine. In the second you will cause the population explosion yourself, by doing something unambiguously good. In the third you have to close the gap you opened. In the fourth you discover why that takes so long.
How it works: the years run in short bursts, then the world pauses for a decision — about five times per century. While it is paused you may launch one thing. Watch what it actually does to the curves before you launch the next one.

THE FOUR THINGS YOU JUST DISCOVERED
Deaths fell first. Births fell later. Everyone alive is standing in the gap.
1. A huge population is not the same as a growing one. In Act 1 roughly forty people per thousand were born every year and roughly thirty-seven died. Both numbers were enormous. Population barely moved for a century, because what matters is only ever the difference.
2. You caused the explosion by doing the right thing. Clean water, vaccines, antibiotics, fertiliser. Nobody on Earth would undo any of them. The death curve fell and the birth curve did not follow for a whole generation, because it takes that long for families to notice that children now survive. That delay is the population explosion. It is not a story about people having more babies. Nobody had more babies. Fewer of them died.
3. The fastest way to reduce births is to stop children dying. This is the part that sounds backwards and is not. If four of your six children will die, you have six. If you are confident all of them will live, you have two. Contraception alone moved your birth curve slowly; child survival plus girls' education moved it far faster, because it changed what parents actually wanted. Saving children's lives is not what causes population growth. It is what ends it.
4. You cannot stop instantly. In Act 4 you set fertility to exactly two children per couple and population kept climbing for decades anyway — because the extra babies from twenty-five years earlier were grown up and having babies of their own. A train with the brakes full on still travels a long way. You stop accelerating long before you stop moving.
This is a teaching model, not a forecast. The rates are tuned so the shape is right and the numbers land in roughly the correct place — the real world had wars, famines, a flu pandemic and enormous regional differences, none of which are in here.
