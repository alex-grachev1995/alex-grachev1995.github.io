After the simulation

What actually happened?

The response combined international emissions controls with treatment of damaged waters.

1960s

Researchers connected distant emissions with acid rain. Pollution could travel across borders before reaching forests and lakes. The problem needed more than local air-quality measurements. [1]

1977

Sweden began a state-funded liming trial. Permanent grant funding followed in 1982. Treatment helped counter acidification while pollution controls developed. [2]

1979–85

Countries agreed to cooperate. The Convention on Long-range Transboundary Air Pollution was signed in 1979 and entered into force in 1983. A 1985 protocol required participating countries to cut sulfur emissions or cross-border sulfur flows by at least 30%. [1,3]

Since 1990

Emissions fell substantially. Cleaner fuels, emissions controls, and international commitments helped. UNECE reported in 2024 that sulfur dioxide emissions across its region had fallen by 83% since 1990. That is not the same as an 83% recovery of lakes. [4]

Was the problem solved?

Not completely. Swedish waters have improved, but recovery is slow. Some remain acidified, and treatment continues. Lower emissions do not instantly restore depleted soils or lost populations. [5]

What did it cost?

In 2025, Sweden reported spreading 68,435 tonnes of lime at a cost of 169 million Swedish kronor. [6]

That is one year's liming cost—not the total cost of Europe's pollution controls or decades of recovery. The game's credits are not kronor.

Model notes

Science

  • Catchment means the land draining into a lake. Limestone-rich catchments often neutralize more acidity than thin-soiled granite catchments. Actual geology varies within both countries.
  • Neutralization removes acidity; it does not store acid for a later release. Accessible neutralizing minerals can be consumed.
  • Low pH can harm organisms directly and mobilize aluminium from soils. The aluminium indicator is a qualitative warning, not a concentration calculation.
  • Tall stacks can redistribute pollution without removing it. Wind, deposition, and atmospheric chemistry influence where it goes.
  • Sound effects are interface cues. A low-pH lake does not literally hiss. “Absent” means lost from this modeled lake, not globally extinct.

Game rules

  • These are representative fictional valleys. Each act starts a new scenario; the years are not historical dates.
  • pH curves, reserve units, costs, and transport amounts are illustrative. This is not a calibrated chemical or ecological prediction model.
  • The icons represent sensitive aquatic insects, freshwater crustaceans, minnows, trout, and perch. Tolerances differ between real species and life stages. The thresholds here are teaching rules.
  • Groups are lost after two unfavorable model years and can return after five favorable years, assuming recolonization is possible. An empty row does not mean the lake has no life.
  • Lake limestone is additive. It is consumed by incoming acid and gradually flushed out. It does not replenish the surrounding soil.
  • Negotiation progress is not an emissions reduction. Once the plan is agreed, implementation takes ten model years to reach a 95% reduction. Controls are then assumed to remain operating.
