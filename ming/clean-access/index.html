There is liquid water under the ice. Getting to it is the easy part.
Radar has found more than four hundred lakes underneath the Antarctic ice sheet, kept liquid by the planet's own heat, the pressure of the ice above, and the fact that four kilometres of ice is very good insulation. Some have been sealed off from the sky for millions of years.

You want to know whether anything is alive down there. Radar cannot tell you that. For that you have to make a hole \u2014 and the moment you make a hole, you have a problem that has nothing to do with drilling.

THE DEBRIEF
THE SAME PROBLEM, 630 MILLION KILOMETRES AWAY
Jupiter's moon Europa is a ball of ice with an ocean underneath \u2014 probably deeper than every ocean on Earth put together, kept liquid by tidal squeezing rather than sunlight. No light gets in. If anything lives there, it eats rock chemistry, not sunshine.

It is the same problem you just solved: liquid water, a thick lid, and no way to look without touching. With three differences that change everything.

1 · The lid is a hundred times thicker

Antarctic ice tops out around four kilometres. Europa's shell is estimated at fifteen to twenty-five kilometres, and nobody knows for certain.

2 · You get one attempt, and no one can inspect the equipment again

On the ice sheet you can pull the drill back up, swab it, and check. Once a probe leaves Earth, every sterilisation decision you made is permanent and unverifiable.

3 · It is not just bad science. It is illegal.

Planetary protection rules under the Outer Space Treaty legally restrict how many living organisms a spacecraft may carry to a place that might host life. This is why NASA deliberately flew the Galileo spacecraft into Jupiter in 2003 \u2014 to make certain it could never crash into Europa and contaminate it.

The thing worth taking away: the hardest part of looking for life somewhere new is not getting there. It is being able to prove that what you found is not you. Every subglacial lake we drill on Earth is a rehearsal for a hole in the ice of another world \u2014 and the rehearsals are where we find out which mistakes are unrecoverable.
