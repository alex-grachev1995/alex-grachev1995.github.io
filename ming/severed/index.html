You have been hired by the province of Kettle Range — a little over 400 square kilometres of forest, two towns, three timber blocks and a mountain ridge.
Nobody will ask you about the animals. There is no box on the form for animals. You are paid on construction cost and travel time — so that is what you should optimise for.
Four species live here. They have been counted. We will count them again in thirty years.
Road-effect zone is how far an animal stays away from a road even when it could physically cross. It is the number that decides everything, and it is the number nobody plots.

CASE FILE 01 · SHAANXI, CHINA
National Road 108 and the Qinling pandas
In the 1970s a highway was cut through continuous panda forest in the Qinling Mountains and split the population in two: roughly 110 pandas on the Xinglongling side and about 20 on the Tianhuashan side. That is your Phase 1, with pandas.

In 1999 a tunnel was completed beneath the ridge, a 13 km stretch of the old surface highway was abandoned, and forest was restored over the top. Pandas have since been recorded in the restored corridor, the gap between the two subgroups is now shorter than a panda walks in a day, and the number of mammal and pheasant species recorded there went from zero to fifteen.

Severance, a stranded subpopulation, and a structural fix — one true story, about 1,200 km from where you are sitting.
CASE FILE 02 · BANFF, ALBERTA
The most-monitored wildlife crossings on Earth
The Trans-Canada Highway through Banff National Park now carries 6 overpasses, 38 underpasses and about 82 km of fencing. Wildlife-vehicle collisions fell by more than 80% — over 96% for elk and deer. More than 200,000 animal crossings have been recorded.

Two details worth more than the headline. First: grizzly crossings went from 7 in 1996 to over 100 by 2006 — animals need years to learn a structure exists, so a crossing that looks like a failure in year two can be a success by year ten. Second: the fencing that nearly eliminated elk deaths did not save the bears; bear mortality roughly doubled as traffic volume doubled.

Which is the whole thesis of this game, confirmed by thirty years of monitoring, on exactly the timescale you have been playing: there is no such thing as a solution for "wildlife." Only solutions for particular animals.
